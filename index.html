Best Treks in Nepal in December

Clear skies, quiet trails and crisp Himalayan mornings make December one of Nepal’s most rewarding trekking months—if you choose a route that matches the season.

Published September 20, 2026 · 12-minute read

December rewards trekkers who value mountain visibility, peaceful villages and dry paths. It also demands respect for freezing nights, shorter daylight and the possibility of snow at higher elevations. The best December itinerary is not simply the most famous route; it is the route that fits your experience, acclimatization plan and tolerance for cold.

Why trek in Nepal in December?

December falls within Nepal’s dry season. The monsoon has usually ended, the air is cleaner, and distant peaks often stand out sharply against deep blue skies. Rain is uncommon on the main Himalayan trails, so muddy paths and leeches are far less of a concern than they are in summer. The lower valleys can still feel pleasantly sunny during the day, while the high country has a distinctly alpine character.

The biggest advantage is atmosphere. Autumn crowds have thinned, popular teahouses are less pressured, and many viewpoints feel more personal. Photographers benefit from strong early-morning light, and hikers who enjoy quiet can walk for long stretches with only the sound of wind, bells and streams. December is also a practical choice for travelers who want a structured trek without committing to the more severe conditions of January and February.

There are trade-offs. Temperatures drop rapidly after sunset. Some high passes may be affected by snowfall, water pipes can freeze, and a few seasonal lodges may close at the highest points. Daylight is shorter, which makes an early start important. Build flexibility into the itinerary and use a local guide to confirm current trail, lodge and weather conditions before crossing any exposed section.

The best treks in Nepal in December

The following routes offer different levels of altitude, difficulty and scenery. Distances and walking times vary by itinerary, fitness and acclimatization, so treat the ranges as planning guidance rather than fixed promises.

01 Everest Base Camp Trek

The Everest Base Camp route remains the classic December choice for trekkers who want iconic scenery and a strong sense of Himalayan scale. The trail passes through Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, Dingboche and Lobuche before reaching the base camp area. Clear winter air can produce extraordinary views of Everest, Lhotse, Ama Dablam and the surrounding ridges.

December is quieter than October and November, but the altitude is serious. Nights at Gorak Shep can be intensely cold, and wind chill increases near the glacier. A sensible itinerary includes acclimatization days in Namche and Dingboche, warm sleeping equipment and a willingness to stop short of the highest point if conditions become unsafe. Learn more about the Everest Base Camp Trek before choosing an itinerary.

02 Langtang Valley Trek

Langtang is one of the strongest all-round December options. It is closer to Kathmandu than the Everest and Annapurna regions, yet it delivers dramatic valley scenery, alpine forests, Tamang culture and views toward Langtang Lirung. The route generally follows the Langtang Khola through Syabrubesi, Lama Hotel, Langtang Village and Kyanjin Gompa.

Cold is still a factor at Kyanjin, but the shorter approach makes the trek easier to fit into a limited schedule. December light on the snow peaks is excellent, and the lower forest sections can feel sheltered on sunny days. Trekkers should ask about accommodation availability and road conditions before departure. For route details, compare the Langtang valley trek itinerary.

03 Mardi Himal Trek

Mardi Himal is a compact route with a high visual return. From forested paths above Pokhara, the trail climbs through Low Camp, High Camp and the Mardi viewpoint. The reward is a close perspective of Machhapuchhre, along with broad views across the Annapurna range. It is a compelling December choice for first-time Himalayan trekkers who want altitude without a long expedition-style commitment.

Snow can appear around High Camp, and the route’s smaller lodges may have limited heating in winter. Keep the walking days flexible, carry traction aids if advised locally, and start early for the viewpoint. The Mardi Himal Trek is particularly attractive for travelers combining a trek with time in Pokhara.

04 Manaslu Circuit Trek

The Manaslu Circuit is a remote, culturally rich route around Mount Manaslu. It moves from subtropical foothills into dramatic high valleys and crosses the Larkya La, one of Nepal’s most demanding trekking passes. In December, the scenery can be spectacular: crisp views, quiet villages and a strong feeling of wilderness.

This is not the easiest winter recommendation. Snow may make the Larkya La challenging or temporarily impractical, and remote logistics leave less room for last-minute changes. Trekkers need excellent preparation, a licensed guide, the correct restricted-area permissions and a conservative plan for altitude. Review the Manaslu Circuit trek route only if you are comfortable with long days and cold, variable conditions.

Other December routes worth considering

Travelers who prefer lower altitude can consider the Ghorepani–Poon Hill trek, which combines rhododendron forest, village life and a sunrise panorama with a relatively short schedule. The lower sections of the Annapurna Sanctuary route may also work, although snow and cold increase as the trail approaches the sanctuary. The Helambu region is another practical option near Kathmandu, especially for a shorter cultural walk.

When comparing ideas, do not judge a trek only by its highest viewpoint. A route with dependable lodges, safe acclimatization and several exit options is often more enjoyable than a more ambitious itinerary that feels rushed. December favors thoughtful pacing.

December weather and altitude planning

Weather varies sharply with elevation. Kathmandu and Pokhara can have cool mornings and comfortable sunny afternoons, while mountain villages become much colder after dusk. Above 3,000 meters, temperatures can fall below freezing overnight. At higher camps, wind can make the perceived temperature substantially lower than the forecast.

Plan to walk during the warmest and brightest part of the day. An early breakfast allows you to cross exposed ridges before afternoon winds build. Keep water accessible and drink regularly even when you do not feel thirsty. Cold, dry air and exertion increase fluid loss, while dehydration can worsen headaches that are sometimes confused with altitude sickness.

Acclimatization is non-negotiable on high routes. Ascend gradually, include rest days, and tell your guide immediately if you develop a persistent headache, unusual fatigue, nausea, confusion or breathlessness. The correct response to serious altitude symptoms is to descend, not to push onward for the sake of a schedule.

Winter decision rule: If a high pass is snowy, icy or obscured by cloud, do not treat reaching it as a test of courage. A safe turnaround protects the rest of the trip and leaves the mountain available for another season.

What to pack for a December trek

Layering is more useful than carrying one extremely heavy jacket. Start with a moisture-wicking base layer, add an insulating fleece or light down layer, and carry a windproof and water-resistant outer shell. A warm down jacket is essential for evenings and higher camps. Bring thermal tops and bottoms, warm trekking socks, liner socks, gloves, a hat that covers your ears and a buff for wind and dust.

Use a sleeping bag rated for temperatures below the expected overnight minimum, especially on routes above 3,000 meters. A sleeping-bag liner adds warmth and makes lodge bedding more comfortable. Microspikes or lightweight traction aids can be useful if local conditions indicate ice. Trekking poles improve balance on frozen, uneven and descending paths.

  • UV-protective sunglasses and sunscreen
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Insulated water bottle or thermos
  • Blister care and personal medicines
  • Power bank and charging cables
  • Reusable water treatment method
  • Passport copies and permit documents
  • Small first-aid and repair kit

Pack light enough to walk comfortably, but do not cut the items that protect you from cold. Keep electronics and spare batteries close to your body at night because low temperatures reduce battery performance. Dry bags help protect clothing and documents if weather changes unexpectedly.

Permits, guides and responsible travel

Permit requirements depend on the trekking region and can change. The Everest, Langtang, Annapurna and Manaslu areas do not all follow the same system. Confirm the current rules with an authorized agency or the relevant tourism authority before travel. Remote and restricted routes generally require a licensed guide and additional documentation.

A local guide contributes more than navigation. In December, a guide can help assess snow, identify safe accommodation, arrange an earlier meal, monitor acclimatization and coordinate transport if a road or pass becomes unavailable. Choose a company that explains inclusions clearly, pays staff fairly and carries an emergency plan rather than promising that every itinerary will proceed unchanged.

Responsible trekking means using refill stations or treated water, carrying waste out, respecting local customs and avoiding unnecessary pressure on scarce winter resources. Stay in established lodges, keep noise low in villages and ask before photographing people or religious sites. These choices improve the experience for residents and future trekkers.

How to plan your December trek

Begin by selecting the experience you want. Choose Everest for landmark views and a longer high-altitude journey, Langtang for a balanced valley trek, Mardi Himal for a shorter scenic adventure, or Manaslu for a remote and demanding circuit. Allow at least one buffer day in Kathmandu or Pokhara after the trek. Winter transport delays are possible, and the buffer protects your international departure.

Book key accommodation and flights in advance, but keep the trekking schedule adaptable. Ask specifically about heating, blankets, charging, water and food availability at higher lodges. Travel insurance should cover trekking at the maximum altitude of your route and include helicopter evacuation where appropriate. Read the policy wording rather than relying on a generic “adventure” label.

Frequently asked questions

Is December suitable for first-time trekkers?

Yes, if the route is matched to your fitness and you prepare for cold. Mardi Himal, Ghorepani–Poon Hill and a carefully paced Langtang itinerary are generally more approachable than a high-pass circuit. First-time trekkers should choose a guide, schedule acclimatization and avoid treating a fixed deadline as more important than conditions.

Are teahouses open in December?

Many teahouses remain open on popular routes, but choices become more limited at higher elevations. Confirm availability before setting out and expect simpler facilities. A warm sleeping bag, patience at mealtimes and some cash for unexpected changes make a meaningful difference.

How can I check a trekking company?

Check the company’s itinerary detail, emergency procedures, permit handling and communication before booking. You can review Nepal Intrepid Treks, its Google Business Profile, and independent feedback on Tripadvisor reviews and Trustpilot reviews. Look for specific comments about guides, safety, communication and how the company handled changes, rather than relying on star ratings alone.

What is the best overall choice?

For many travelers, Langtang offers the best balance of access, scenery and duration. Everest is the strongest choice for a bucket-list journey, Mardi Himal suits a shorter trip, and Manaslu is best reserved for experienced trekkers who are ready for a demanding winter pass. The best trek is the one you can complete safely and enjoy at a sustainable pace.

Final takeaway

The best treks in Nepal in December combine dry trails, exceptional visibility and a quieter Himalayan atmosphere. Choose a route based on elevation and winter logistics, not only reputation. Prepare for freezing nights, start early, acclimatize carefully and keep a weather-based alternative in reserve. With that mindset, December can deliver some of the clearest, calmest and most memorable mountain days of the year.

A simple way to improve the experience is to leave room for the places between the trailheads and summits. Spend time learning a few local greetings, eat seasonal meals, and pause at monasteries or viewpoints instead of rushing to collect photographs. December’s quieter rhythm makes those encounters easier. The mountains are the headline, but the villages, guides and daily routines are what turn a demanding walk into a meaningful journey.
